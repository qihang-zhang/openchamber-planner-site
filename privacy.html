Privacy policy

OpenChamber Planner · effective 10 November 2026

In short

The Planner runs on your own machine, as a service of your OpenChamber, and talks to Google's APIs directly from there. Its developers run no Planner server and receive nothing you allow it to read: no copies of your data, no analytics, no advertising, and nothing is sold. Google data leaves that machine only for the places listed under Where your data can go, and only when you set them up or start them yourself.

Signing in

You sign in on Google's own page, so the Planner never sees your Google password. Google shows the access the Planner asks for, and you can allow or refuse each part. The Planner asks only for the scopes of the features you choose, and asks for Drive and for Gmail actions only when you first use them.

What it keeps, and where

  • Your Google access. The refresh token Google grants is stored on the machine that runs the Planner, in a file only your user account can read, outside the plan's data. It is never sent to the Planner's web page or to anyone else.
  • What it shows. Messages, events, tasks, files and contacts are read from Google when the Planner needs them, held briefly in the service's memory to keep the panel fast, and not written to disk.
  • What you turn into a task. The task keeps what it needs in your plan: its title (for example, an email's subject), its notes (for example, the sender and Gmail's snippet, which you can edit) and a link back to the item in Google. The plan is a set of files in a git repository on the machine that runs the Planner.
  • Bookkeeping. Small files beside the plan, outside its synced data and readable only by your user account, record for example which invitations it declined for you (kept for a month) and which reminders it has shown.

Where your data can go

  1. To your browser. The Planner's panel is part of OpenChamber's web page. What it shows goes from the service to the browser you open OpenChamber in, which may be on another device when you reach your OpenChamber over a network. Use HTTPS or a private network for that connection.
  2. To a sync remote you choose (off unless you set one). With Settings → Sync, the plan, including the titles, notes and links of tasks made from Google items, is pushed to the git remote you name, so your other machines can share it. Your Google tokens are never part of it.
  3. To an AI agent, only when you start one. When you hand a task to an OpenCode agent, or use Ask Planner, Braindump or the Daily highlights summary, the Planner puts the text of the tasks involved (which can include an email's subject, sender and snippet) in an OpenCode chat. OpenCode sends that chat to the AI model provider you configured in OpenCode, under your own agreement with that provider. The Planner never starts this by itself, never gives an agent your Google access, and never uses Google data to train AI models.
  4. To Google. The Planner sends Google only the requests that the features above need.

Nothing else: no Google data goes to the Planner's developers or to any other party, and the developers never see it.

Limited Use

The Planner's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements, and to the Google Workspace API User Data and Developer Policy. Google data is used only for the features described above. It is not sold, not used for advertising, not used to determine credit-worthiness, and not used to train AI models.

Security

  • Every request to Google uses HTTPS.
  • Your tokens and the bookkeeping files are readable only by your user account on the machine that runs the Planner.
  • The Planner's web page never receives your Google tokens; the service makes every request to Google itself.

Removing access and deleting data

  • In the Planner, Settings → Google → Remove disconnects an account, revokes its access at Google and deletes its token from your machine. You can also revoke the access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Tasks you made from Google items stay in your plan until you delete them. The plan keeps a history of its changes, so a deleted task's earlier text stays in that history (and in your sync remote, if you set one) until you delete the Planner's data folder, ~/.local/share/openchamber-planner/, and the remote.
  • To remove everything, remove your Google accounts in the Planner, then delete that folder.

Children

The Planner is not directed to children under 13.

Changes

A new version of this policy is posted here with a new effective date. If the Planner starts using Google data in a way not described here, it asks for your agreement first.
